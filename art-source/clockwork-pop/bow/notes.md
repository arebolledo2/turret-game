# Clockwork bow — frozen iteration 3

Three harness runs used; all three passed the export contract. Final files are `cp_bow.glb`, `cp_bow.blend`, and `build_cp_bow.py`; their hashes and the reviewed iteration are recorded in `frozen_export.json`. No files outside this run were changed. No other runs were read.

The reference is an upright recurved coral bow with turquoise bindings, brass tip rollers, a drawn black string, ivory spearhead, turquoise receiver with a brass front frame and purple mechanical details, large rear brass wind-up spool, round side inspection medallion and an octagonal teal/brass pedestal. The construction preserves these parts and their relative silhouette. Unseen reverse details are symmetric or mechanically plausible inventions.

Iteration 1 established the construction, but the lower string passed behind the support and fletching hid inside the receiver. It also spent 14,352 triangles on small bevels. Iteration 2 shifted the bow forward, adjusted the arrow nock and taut string attachment, exposed ivory fins near the projectile's rear, deepened the coral/teal palette and reduced small bevel tessellation to 9,492 triangles. Iteration 3 opened the bow grip around the shaft and added a slim support rail beneath it; removing the arrow therefore leaves the bow attached to the receiver. String runs from each brass limb tip to the arrow nock inside the receiver. The section entering the casing is deliberately occluded, while both exterior spans read clearly in side and three-quarter views.

Final validation: **9,688 triangles, 14 mesh objects, 8 used materials, 0 textures, 0 animations**. Size is **0.713 × 1.794 × 1.631 m**, lowest point **0.000 m**. `Aim` is an identity-rotation empty on the actual central turret axis; `Muzzle` is a bare marker under Aim at the forward spearhead tip. `LoadedArrow` is one separate mesh directly under Aim and can be hidden or removed independently. The pedestal and support are static. `contract.json` uses `origin_node: "Aim"` because the forward arrow and bow create genuinely asymmetric total bounds.

The triangle target is advisory and exceeded to retain smooth recurved thick ribbons, round spool flanges, machined receiver edges, brass cap profiles, side medallions and small hardware. Small bevels were simplified without damaging those silhouettes. Object/material targets are met. The native runtime integration, motion controller and Godot import are delegated to the parent task and have not been tested here.

Remaining visual limitations: the checker's intense studio lighting makes teal and brass paler than the reference; the coral is less saturated in some lit views. The receiver is more box-like than the reference, and its internal clutch is simplified. The support rail is a functional invention absent from the visible concept. Rear fletching was added as requested, and sits in the rear portion of the visible shaft ahead of the enclosure so it remains readable. From a straight-on front view the upright bow is intentionally edge-on; its recognizable recurved silhouette is best at elevated oblique gameplay views. No procedural texture or AO baking was added, so painted surfaces are cleaner and flatter than an authored texture pass.

Honest self-score: **7.8/10** for reference fidelity and functional construction. A polished game-ready geometry candidate; native-game acceptance still belongs to the parent task.

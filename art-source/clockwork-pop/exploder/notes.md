## Appearance and procedural surfaces

Coral pressure orb with dark structural recesses, twelve curved shell panels and six brass hollow pressure plugs. Thick teal top cap with rolled lip/rounded crown, brass neck and pressure pin. Large cream-framed orange/red convex optic, teal/amethyst indicator jewels, and small underside coral bulb. The unseen rear receives a modest teal/brass service cap.

All three images are generated by the builder with seeded numpy operations, saved beside it, packed into the Blend and embedded in the GLB. A coral enamel image supplies clean broad variation and subtle enamel grain. A shared finish atlas serves teal, brass, ivory, dark recesses and amethyst. The dedicated eye image supplies red/orange gradient, radial jewel facets and painted highlights. Every mesh has UVs. Materials are opaque; the optic uses an opaque polished finish with gentle emission rather than stacked transparent glass.

## Remaining limitations

- The illustration's panel divisions are irregular; this model uses a more regular spherical layout. Its face seam is thinner than the illustrated broad recess.
- The pressure fittings are hollow geometry, although frontal studio reflections can make their brass interiors appear bright.
- The teal cap has a simpler lower bowl than the illustrated dark segmented cup. Shell and optic colours vary under the checker lighting; the optic is opaque rather than transmissive.
- The rear is invented from a single-view reference. Fine enamel grain and optical facet detail become subtle at game distance.
- No baked ambient occlusion, normal/roughness texture maps or animation clips. Game lighting, hover motion, collision and crowd testing are owned by parent integration.
- No game files were touched; Godot verification is delegated to the parent.

Honest visual self-score: **8/10**. The main pressure-orb silhouette, coral/teal/brass palette and orange optic are present with a compact single-body hierarchy. The seams and cap treatment are interpretations rather than an exact reconstruction of the painting.

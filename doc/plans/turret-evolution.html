TURRET / SURVIVOR · DESIGN PROPOSAL 0310 OCTOBER 2026 · FOR REVIEW
Scarcity • Salvage • Evolution

Fewer turrets.
More attachment.

Build a small squad worth caring about. Gather scrap for purchases, leave some behind to grow your turrets, and take on dangerous build zones for exceptional rewards.

The recommendation

Raise the price of expansion. Give each turret two meaningful evolutions. Replace easy free turrets with optional, contested build encounters.

The main balancing risk is squeezing shop money and early damage at the same time. Ship the economy and evolution together, then tune zone difficulty.

01 / Intent

Invest in the squad you have.

Expansion, evolution and items should be three competing ways to become stronger. A turret purchase should feel substantial; an evolution should change how an individual turret fights.

1. Fight & positionCarry a turret, shape firing lanes, decide which scrap to collect.
2. Resolve salvageUncollected scrap becomes proximity-weighted turret XP.
3. Evolve & shopChoose earned forms for free, then spend collected money.
4. Take a riskPursue a tougher build encounter during the next wave.

What the code does today

All leftover scrap is banked. The same leftovers also add build-zone slots and improve zone quality next wave. A zone takes 6 seconds at tier 0 and only 3.3 seconds at tier 3; its default reward roll is 65% turret.

Turret prices already rise by wave and by paid purchases. The actual purchase coefficient is +50%; two older comments still say +25%.

What changes together

Remove automatic banking of leftover scrap and its next-wave zone bonuses. Add turret XP, stronger expansion pricing, and per-turret evolution in one playable slice.

Keep the starting turret, the 20 starting scrap, existing item prices, and the 5 × wave completion bonus for the first balance pass. That bonus remains spendable money.

02 / Scarcity

A new turret is a commitment.

Start with double the turret base price and a steeper squad-growth curve. Keep the existing one turret + three item shop layout, with no hard turret cap.

Proposed price = floor(base × 2.0 × (1 + 0.20 × wave) × (1 + 0.65 × N + 0.10 × N²) × discounts)

N = additional turrets acquired this run, excluding the starting turret. Count shop purchases and free build-zone frames; evolutions do not count. Track acquisitions independently of living count so future selling or destruction cannot reset prices. Ordinary discounts still apply; retain a positive minimum price.

Keep expansion possible

An Ion starts at 19 scrap after wave 1, versus 9 today. With one additional turret already acquired, it would cost 44 after wave 3. Target a second turret around waves 2–4, then a squad of 4–6 by wave 20. These are tuning goals, not simulated outcomes.

Close the free-turret bypass

Build encounters mostly award XP or an item. A rare frame contract can add one base turret, at most once per run. It still increments N. Rerolls can change the offer but never reduce its expansion scaling.

Economy items and recovery rules

Keep turret-scaling items useful by applying their existing penalty modifiers to the new linear coefficient (0.65), provisionally clamped at 0.40; leave the quadratic term intact. Audit flat discounts and every-third-purchase discounts together, rather than allowing unbounded reductions to erase scarcity.

Keep a separate paid-purchase counter for an every-third-purchase effect. The expansion counter includes free frames, but receiving a frame should not consume a paid discount. Show final prices; do not expose these internal counters as shop tags.

Before tuning up enemy health, test runs that buy items and evolve only their starter. If the first few waves become a damage or repair trap, ease early turret prices or XP thresholds rather than quietly awarding more free frames.

03 / Salvage

Left on the floor. Invested in a turret.

Each uncollected scrap unit becomes 1 XP at a successful wave end. Randomly choose its recipient, with a strong but non-exclusive preference for nearby turrets.

weightᵢ = 0.15 + 1 / (1 + (distanceᵢ / 4 m)²)
probabilityᵢ = weightᵢ / sum(all eligible weights)

Use planar distance from each pickup’s final position to each turret’s position at the wave-end snapshot. Roll per scrap unit, not per pickup object, so one 10-scrap pickup behaves like ten 1-scrap pickups. Batch the visual trails, not the accounting. The small uniform floor keeps distant turrets in the draw.

A clean money / growth choice

Collected scrap uses normal currency multipliers. Leftovers use raw scrap value for XP, with no cash bonus or duplicate reward. The wave-completion bonus remains money. Remove the existing leftover-to-zone-tier and extra-slot rules.

New recommendation: an accessible Auto-pull on/off control. Off disables magnet attraction but walking onto scrap still collects it. Show the state clearly; audit controller bindings before assigning a button.

Fair distribution rules

Every owned, non-maxed turret is eligible, including the carried turret at its actual carried position before it is returned to the ground. There is no kill-credit preference. More turrets naturally split the XP supply.

Exclude turrets once they have all XP needed for their final form, including banked XP. If none can accept XP—or there are no turrets—bank that otherwise-unusable remainder through the normal money path and report it separately.

Settlement order, randomness and edge cases
  1. Enter WAVE_END once, freeze simulation, and snapshot only uncollected, unconsumed pickups and eligible turret IDs/positions. Take this snapshot before clear_field resets actors or drops a carried turret.
  2. Allocate units using a dedicated run-seeded salvage RNG stream. Remove a recipient from the weighted pool when its remaining XP capacity reaches zero. Show expected bias, never promise an exact recipient.
  3. Consume each pickup exactly once, store XP, and queue eligible promotions. Money pickups and XP pickups are mutually exclusive. Duplicate wave-end callbacks cannot award either twice.
  4. Clear combat, return the carried turret, grant the normal wave bonus, then open the Shop with the evolution queue. Do not call pickup.collect() for XP-consumed scrap.
  5. Defeat, restart and exit discard unsettled scrap. Victory still resolves XP and reports earned promotions, without forcing branch choices for a run that has ended. New runs reset all turret XP and forms.

Use separate telemetry for cash collected, cash bonus, XP allocated and overflow salvage. A visual animation skip must never skip accounting. A turret spawned during the final combat tick is eligible only if it exists in the frozen snapshot.

The pickup-magnet tradeoff becomes real. More collection can mean less evolution. Preserve the player’s ability to turn attraction off, explain this in item inspection, and test the controls before pricing magnet upgrades as unconditionally positive.
04 / Individual progression

Every level earns a new form.

L1

Base chassis
Purchased or built. 0 XP. A clear, useful attack identity.

L2

Specialization · 30 XP total
Choose one of two branches. New silhouette, name and attack behavior.

L3

Mastery · 120 XP total
90 more XP. Evolve along the chosen branch into its definitive form.

Free evolution, meaningful choice

Level thresholds are checked at wave end. Evolution costs no scrap: the opportunity cost was leaving money behind. Limit each turret to one promotion per wave; bank overflow toward the next form.

Pick the L2 branch in the full-screen Shop before Deploy. Resolve multiple ready turrets one at a time, with a clear remaining count. L3 follows that branch automatically; show its transformation in the summary.

Keep the same turret ID, location, entrenchment state and ownership. Carrying does not pause XP eligibility. Aim for the first evolution around waves 3–5 and mastery around waves 8–12 for an intentionally fed turret.

Make the mechanical changes substantial and readable. At equal investment, use roughly +25–40% effective output per evolution as an initial comparison budget, including reach, coverage, stagger and burn—not a universal damage multiplier. Gatling ramp-up, wide spreads and heavy reloads should express distinct roles; avoid attaching arbitrary stat penalties to every branch.

Readable at a glance

Base: one strong silhouette. L2: a new barrel, limb or emitter. L3: a distinctive moving mechanism, stronger accents and clearer attack effects. Keep a directional muzzle where the weapon fires directionally.

Show each individual

The current Shop groups turrets by type. Add individual portraits with form, level and XP. A focused card previews its actual upgrade and affected attacks. No “tall”, “wide” or internal turret tags.

Preserve item logic

Items still target Projectile, AoE, Instant and Kinetic. A hybrid resolves each attack separately: a gunblade’s bullet gets Projectile bonuses; its swing gets Kinetic bonuses. Shared effects apply once per relevant hit.

New bases where the playstyle is new.

06 / Optional encounters

Earn the reward. Don’t just stand still.

Make build zones rarer, contested and readable. Difficulty should come from committing to a dangerous position while managing the wave, with enough room to dodge.

Claim, clear, then charge

Claim: a 2-second warning shows the exact reward and the encounter’s danger. Enter to activate it.

Clear: defeat a small marked guard pack. Use existing enemy behaviors and telegraphed spawns; begin with 3–5 normal-equivalent guards, including a charger at higher tiers.

Charge: stay within the existing 1.6 m radius for 10 / 12 / 14 seconds by tier. Enemies inside the zone contest it and pause progress. You can carry and fire a turret to defend the site.

Difficult without being brittle

Charge has checkpoints at one-third and two-thirds. Leaving holds progress for 0.75 seconds, then drains only the unfinished segment at 8% per second. Damage pauses charge for 0.75 seconds; it does not erase a completed checkpoint.

The wave keeps advancing. Skipping or failing the encounter has no additional penalty. Never require standing motionless or tanking damage to finish it.

Do not solve difficulty by making rewards tedious. Longer channel time alone encourages camping, while a tiny radius punishes dodging. Contesting enemies, a finite guard fight and a clear deadline make the encounter demand a strong build and deliberate movement.
Encounter fairness, reward reliability and Convoy

Guards are a bounded encounter budget layered over the wave, capped provisionally at 10% of its normal-equivalent threat. They grant no scrap or turret XP themselves; the advertised reward pays for the encounter. Tag their telemetry separately, and prevent reward farming by re-entering the zone.

Show warning / guarded / contested / charging states with the polished dial and concise text. Do not spawn inside terrain blockers or immediately under the hero. Evaluate turret coverage as well as simple distance; if no fair position is found, skip this opportunity rather than forcing a bad one.

Items must remain eligible under stack limits. A frame is delivered at a validated reserved spot near the completed zone, or a visible pending reward is settled into valid placement at the next Shop. Never silently discard a completed reward. If the map has no room, offer an explicit conversion to a training cache.

In Core mode, avoid forcing a choice between an impossible defense and a mandatory reward. In Convoy, use remaining route time as well as the timer, place sites beside the upcoming route, and skip opportunities too close to arrival. On short custom runs, scale the schedule; do not silently require a 20-wave run to see the feature.

Frame contracts can appear from wave 10 onward; earlier frame rolls become training. All rewards are revealed before commitment. The cap and forecast-time check are more reliable scarcity controls than an unrestricted spawn chance.

07 / Implementation plan

Prove the progression before growing the roster.

The first slice should establish that four interesting turrets are more fun than ten interchangeable ones. Build the shared rules once, then expand attack behaviors and models.

Progression foundation + economy

Add per-instance XP, level and form state keyed by the existing turret ID. Introduce atomic wave-end salvage settlement and a dedicated RNG stream. Replace leftover banking/zone bonuses, update turret pricing and audit economy items.

Exit: replayable settlement, exact scrap conservation, no duplicated rewards, and a tunable economy report. Keep this behind a coordinated feature flag until evolution is playable.

Playable evolution slice

Ship Ion → Machine Gun → Gatling and Ion → Scattershot → Crossfire; Bat → Saber → Twin Saber and Bat → Gunblade → Repeater Blade. Add attack-level stat resolution, Shop evolution choices, individual XP displays, pickup-attraction control and modular art changes.

Exit: all four branches behave distinctly, carried attacks work, mixed-family items affect only the correct attack, and both cash-heavy and XP-heavy runs remain viable.

During this development-only slice, restrict its test catalog and starting turret to Ion/Bat. The normal roster stays available with the feature off; do not release a progression mode where three stocked bases cannot evolve.

Complete existing bases + encounter pass

Give Plasma, Shock and Tesla their paths. Replace the old build-zone rules with the guarded encounter and reward caps. Ship the combined progression mode only after all five existing bases support it.

Exit: all supported modes and difficulty presets have reachable encounters, readable failure states, and a useful item shop alongside evolution.

Expand in mechanic order

Add Bow first, reusing projectile spread and pierce. Then Bowling with rolling collision and bounded knockback. Add Flame after the DoT pipeline is tested. Reserve bouncing balls and more exotic capstones for a later content pass.

Exit: each new base earns its shop slot through a distinct combat role. Produce final 2D concepts and 3D conversions after the prototype proves that role.

Acceptance checks

  • Collected cash and allocated XP never double-count a unit; pending queue-free pickups are excluded.
  • Near and far recipients match weighted probabilities across fixed-seed samples; equidistant turrets are fair.
  • Carry/drop, overflow, final form, no recipients, victory, defeat, restart and repeated finish callbacks settle correctly.
  • Each evolution is a real form change; identity and item effects survive it. Multi-level overflow promotes once per wave.
  • Spread, hybrid and burn attacks cannot multiply modifiers or proc triggers accidentally.
  • All forms fire while carried and hit small enemies. Large shots and bowling balls use appropriate swept shapes.
  • Zones cannot be farmed, spawned too late, or completed twice; rewards survive temporary placement blockage.
  • Controller-only Shop, evolution choices and attraction control work; reduced motion preserves all information.

Playtest targets, not promises

  • Second turret around waves 2–4; 4–6 total by wave 20 for a typical balanced run.
  • A deliberately fed turret specializes around waves 3–5 and reaches mastery around waves 8–12.
  • Players continue buying useful items instead of saving every coin for frames.
  • Collect-most and feed-most strategies both finish comparable seeded runs without a mandatory opening.
  • Committed build-zone attempts succeed roughly 50–70% on the standard preset; record skips separately.
  • At most one free frame per run; most strength comes from purchases, items and evolved existing turrets.
  • Measure damage, coverage, proc rate and frame time at equal total investment—not only raw damage per shot.
Balance the coupled systems together. The price sandbox is arithmetic, not a run simulation. Use scripted collection policies and seeded playtests to compare money, repairs, item purchases, turret count and evolution timing before calling the numbers final.
Why this shape, and which alternatives stay open?

Why three forms? Every level can afford a visible mechanical transformation. More numerical levels would dilute that promise and multiply art work.

Why weighted random instead of nearest-only? It follows the requested behavior and rewards positioning without letting one nearby turret absorb everything deterministically. Keep the bias radius configurable; 4 m is the proposed default.

Why no hard squad cap? Prices and split XP already impose opportunity costs. Measure whether those controls work before adding an arbitrary slot ceiling.

Why mostly training rewards from build zones? It makes difficult encounters strengthen scarce turrets without reopening the free-frame flood. If zones should remain primarily turret sources, keep the rarity schedule and once-per-run frame cap, and make the remaining rewards items.

Decisions to review: the 2× base / accelerating acquisition curve; three forms with one branch choice; the Auto-pull toggle; and build zones shifting primarily toward XP rewards. Everything here is a recommendation, not an accepted implementation requirement.
